# Google Business Profile Strategy — Jeremy Poole Johnson

**Why this document exists:** GBP is the highest-ROI channel in the entire plan, and it's the one that works *before* the website exists. Ten real reviews will out-earn every other piece of SEO work combined.

**It can go live this week.** It does not wait for the site.

## The decision that gates everything: listing type

This depends on the delivery model, which is still unconfirmed with Jeremy. Two paths, and they behave differently:

### Path A — Students come to his studio (address-based listing)

- The address **must be displayed publicly.** Google's rules: if customers come to you, you show where.
- **Stronger ranking.** Proximity to the searcher is a major factor, and an address anchors him properly in the map pack.
- Downside: his home address is on the internet.

### Path B — He travels to students (Service Area Business)

- Address can be **hidden**; he defines a service radius or list of suburbs instead.
- **Weaker ranking** — Google anchors SABs less precisely.
- Correct choice only if he genuinely travels to them.

> **Don't fudge this.** Setting up as an SAB to hide the address while actually teaching from home is a policy violation, and home-based businesses already get suspended more than most. Suspension appeals are slow and miserable.

**If it's a home studio and he's uncomfortable publishing the address:** the honest options are (a) accept it — thousands of home teachers do, (b) genuinely operate as travel-to-student, or (c) rent a room somewhere. There is no clever fourth option, and pretending otherwise is how listings get killed.

### Online lessons

Online-only is **not eligible** for a GBP listing at all. This reinforces the strategy call: online is a convenience for local students, not a separate market.

## Setup checklist

### 1. Business name — do not keyword-stuff

Use his real trading name. `Jeremy Poole Johnson` or `Jeremy Poole Johnson Piano Studio`.

**Not** `Jeremy Poole Johnson Jazz Piano Lessons [City]`. That's a direct policy violation, competitors actively report it, and the penalty is suspension. The category and services fields are where keywords legitimately live.

### 2. Categories — the single biggest on-profile ranking lever

- **Primary:** the most specific one available — likely **"Piano Instructor"**
- **Secondary:** "Music Instructor", "Music School", "Musician" as applicable

Check what's actually in the category picker rather than assuming; Google's list shifts. **The primary category does most of the work** — pick the most specific match, not the broadest.

### 3. Verification

Usually **video verification** now. He'll need to show, in one unbroken recording:

- The location — street, house number, the room
- **Tools of the trade** — the piano, sheet music, the teaching setup
- Proof he manages the place — mail with his name, keys, letting himself in

This trips people up constantly. Plan the walk-through before hitting record. If it fails, appeals are possible but slow — so do it properly once.

### 4. Hours — set to his *actual* after-hours availability

This is a genuine differentiator. Someone searching at 8pm sees a piano teacher who's open.

**Only list hours he can really do.** Listing aspirational hours produces disappointed callers and no-shows on slots he needs.

### 5. Services — free, underused keyword real estate

GBP lets him list services with descriptions and prices. Almost no local teacher fills this in. He should list, each with a price:

| Service | Note |
|---|---|
| **First Lesson (45 min)** | The paid trial — half rate |
| **Adult Beginner Piano Lessons** | The lead segment |
| **Jazz Piano & Improvisation** | The wedge |
| **Piano Lessons for Children & Teens** | Secondary segment |

This reinforces the published-pricing strategy and puts his target keywords on the profile legitimately.

### 6. Booking link

GBP supports an appointment URL. **Point it straight at the Cal.com booking page** — not the homepage. Removes a click from the highest-intent action on the profile.

### 7. Messaging — turn it on

Same logic as preferring SMS over email: a shy adult will tap "message" who would never compose an email.

**Only if he'll respond quickly** — Google displays response time, and a slow one is worse than none.

### 8. Description

750 characters. Lead with the adult-returner positioning, not the CV. Mention jazz, adults, beginners, and the suburb naturally.

### 9. NAP consistency

Name, address and phone **identical** across the website, GBP, and any directory listing. Basic, boring, and it matters.

## Reviews — this is the whole game

Everything else on this page is setup. Reviews are the engine.

### The thresholds

| Count | Effect |
|---|---|
| **0–5** | Effectively invisible in the map pack |
| **~10** | The point where things start working |
| **25+** | Strong for a local service business in this category |

### Velocity beats volume

**Two reviews a month forever beats twenty in one week and then nothing.** Google reads recency and steady flow. A burst looks manufactured and the effect decays.

### The ask — timing is everything

Not a generic email blast. The moment is **right after a breakthrough** — they've just played a whole tune for the first time, they're delighted, they're standing there with their coat on.

> "That was genuinely great today. Would you mind leaving me a Google review? Takes two minutes — I'll text you the link now."

Then **send the short review link by SMS before they've left.** Email asks get ignored; SMS asks get done in the car.

### Getting useful review *content*

He can't dictate what people write, but he can legitimately prompt:

> "If you write one — it really helps if you mention what you were nervous about beforehand and what you're playing now."

That produces reviews mentioning *adult beginner*, *nervous*, *hadn't played in years*, *jazz*, *patient* — which is exactly what the next anxious 45-year-old needs to read, and it helps him surface for those terms.

### Seeding the first ten

From **existing and former private students.** Past students from years back count and are usually happy to help.

> ⚠️ **Not current music school students.** That's the non-solicitation risk flagged in STRATEGY.md, and a public review trail is exactly how an employer would notice.

### Reply to every single review

Ranking signal, and it reads well to anyone browsing. Warm and specific, not "Thanks for the 5 stars!" Reply to negative ones calmly and factually — a well-handled bad review builds more trust than an unbroken wall of fives.

### What he must never do

- ❌ **Incentivise reviews** — discounts, free lessons, anything. Against Google policy and, in many jurisdictions, consumer law
- ❌ **Gate them** — asking only the happy ones is a policy violation
- ❌ **Buy them** or have friends who aren't students write them
- ❌ Review-swap with other teachers

## Photos & video

Profiles with more photos get materially more engagement, and **recency matters** — add a few every month rather than dumping thirty once.

**What to upload:**

- **The actual room they'd sit in.** This does the same anxiety-reducing job as the welcome video — they can see it's a nice space with a real piano, not intimidating
- Him at the piano
- Him teaching (with permission)
- The exterior / street view if students come to him — genuinely helps them find the door
- The piano itself, close up

**Video:** GBP accepts short clips (~30s). **Upload a cutdown of the welcome video.** Practically no local competitor does this, and it's the single warmest thing on the profile.

> **Skip geotagging photos.** Google strips EXIF. It's a persistent myth and a waste of an afternoon.

## Q&A — free FAQ, almost nobody uses it

**He can ask *and answer* his own questions.** This is explicitly allowed and it's one of the most underused features on the platform.

Seed these, then answer each properly:

- *"Do you teach adults who haven't played since childhood?"*
- *"Do I need to be able to read music?"*
- *"Do you teach complete beginners?"*
- *"What are your rates?"*
- *"When are lessons available?"*
- *"Do you teach jazz and improvisation?"*

These appear directly in the knowledge panel and do real conversion work before anyone reaches the website.

## Google Posts

Roughly monthly. Low direct ranking value, but they show activity and appear in the panel.

Best use is **availability**, which creates genuine urgency:

> *"Two Tuesday evening slots open from March."*

Posts expire after about a week, so don't over-invest — one a month is fine. Don't let this become a chore that crowds out review-collection, which matters far more.

## Measurement

GBP Performance gives him the numbers that matter:

| Metric | What it tells him |
|---|---|
| **Direct searches** (his name) | Word of mouth is working |
| **Discovery searches** (category) | SEO/GBP is working |
| Calls, website clicks, booking clicks | Actual intent |
| Direction requests | Local pull |

Watching **direct vs discovery** over time is the clearest read on which channel is actually feeding him — and it's the thing the website's own analytics cannot tell him.

## Realistic timeline

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Create, verify, categories, hours, services, photos, description |
| **Weeks 2–6** | Seed the first 5–10 reviews from existing/former private students |
| **Months 2–3** | Listing starts appearing for category searches. Q&A seeded, photos added monthly |
| **Months 3–6** | Map pack presence within a few km. Review flow becomes routine |
| **Ongoing** | 1–2 new reviews a month, forever |

**A new listing takes weeks to months to rank.** Reviews accelerate this more than anything else available.

## The limitation worth understanding

GBP ranks heavily on **proximity to the searcher.** He'll do well within a few kilometres and poorly beyond it, and there is no legitimate way around that.

That's precisely why the website's organic pages still matter — they reach the people outside the map-pack radius who are willing to travel for a specialist jazz teacher. **The two channels cover different ground; neither replaces the other.**

---

## Suspension risks — avoid all of these

- Keyword-stuffing the business name
- A virtual office, mailbox, or a co-working address he doesn't operate from
- SAB setup while customers actually visit him
- Listing hours he isn't available
- Any incentivised or fake reviews

Keep the verification evidence — mail, photos, the video — in case he ever needs to appeal.

## Priority order

1. **Get it verified** — nothing else happens until this is done
2. **Reviews, relentlessly** — the whole ballgame
3. Categories and services filled properly
4. Photos and the welcome-video cutdown
5. Q&A seeded
6. Booking link pointed at Cal.com
