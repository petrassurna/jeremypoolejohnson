# Video Brief — Jeremy Poole Johnson

Everything he needs to shoot, in priority order. Scripts are drafts to speak in his own words, not lines to read.

**Audience for all of it:** an adult, 30–60, who played as a kid, quit, and is slightly embarrassed to be looking. Browsing on a phone, at night, probably with the sound off.

---

## The one rule that governs everything

> **Do not open with virtuosity.**

The instinct is to lead with the most impressive playing possible. For this audience it backfires — it *raises* the intimidation barrier. They're already worried they'll be judged by a professional.

**Permission first. Credibility second.** He needs to look like someone who'd be kind about a wrong note, and *then* like someone worth learning from.

Second rule: talk about **them**, not about him. Every sentence that starts "I've performed with…" is a sentence not doing the job.

# Tier 1 — the four videos the site needs to launch

## Video 1 — The Welcome
**60–90s · homepage hero · the highest-converting asset on the entire site**

Structure:

| Beat | Seconds |
|---|---|
| Play something warm (a ballad, *not* a burner), then stop and turn to camera | 0–8 |
| Name the fear immediately | 8–25 |
| What he actually does differently | 25–55 |
| The jazz credential, lightly | 55–65 |
| The invitation | 65–85 |

**Draft script:**

> *[plays ~8 seconds of something warm — then stops, turns to camera]*
>
> Hi, I'm Jeremy.
>
> Most of the adults I teach haven't touched a piano in twenty years. A lot of them quit when they were about twelve and always felt a bit sad about it. If that's you — you're in the right place. And you're not too old. That's genuinely not a thing.
>
> Here's what I actually do. We don't start with scales, and there are no exams. I ask you what you'd love to be able to play, and we start working towards it — usually with chords, so you're playing something that sounds like *music* in the first few weeks, not in year three.
>
> I play jazz for a living, so if you want to improvise, or finally work out what those chord symbols above the stave actually mean — that's my home ground. But mostly I just want to get you playing.
>
> The first lesson's 45 minutes. We'll have a play, work out what you're after, and see if we get on. No obligation at all.
>
> Come and have a go.

**Direction:** shoot this *after* Video 3, once he's warmed up and relaxed. Take 5 will be better than take 1. Warm, unhurried, slightly amused. If he sounds like he's presenting, go again.

## Video 2 — The First Month Demo
**60s · proves the promise is real**

This one collapses the gap between *"I can't play"* and *"I could play that."* Concrete beats abstract every time.

**Recommendation: the 12-bar blues.** Three chords, sounds instantly good, genuinely playable in a month, and it's jazz-adjacent so it's on-brand. Nothing else demos this well.

**Draft script:**

> This is three chords. That's it — three.
>
> *[plays each slowly, naming them]*
>
> Put them in this order, and…
>
> *[plays a chorus of blues]*
>
> That's a twelve-bar blues. Most of my adult students are playing this inside about a month.
>
> And once you've got it, you can start doing this over the top —
>
> *[30 seconds of tasteful, restrained improvising over the same changes]*
>
> Same three chords. That's where it gets fun.

**Direction:** the improv at the end must stay *restrained*. If it turns into a display, it undoes the video's whole job. Show them a door, not a mountain.

## Video 3 — Him Playing
**~90s · one take · no talking**

A standard, played beautifully. This is where credibility lives.

**Choose recognisable over impressive.** *Misty*, *Body and Soul*, *Someone to Watch Over Me*, *Autumn Leaves*. The reaction we want is *"I want to be able to play that"* — which needs them to know the tune.

**Placement: below the welcome video on the page.** Never above it.

**Direction:** fixed camera, wide enough to see hands *and* face. One continuous take — no cuts. Shoot this first as a warm-up.

---

## Video 4 — Adult Student Testimonial
**45–60s · peer proof**

Someone roughly the viewer's age saying *"I was terrified, it was fine."* Against fear, peer proof beats authority proof.

**Ask off-camera, let them ramble, cut to 45s:**

- What did you think before you started?
- Were you nervous about it?
- What can you play now that you couldn't a year ago?
- What would you say to someone who's thinking about it but feels a bit embarrassed?

The fourth question is the one that produces the usable clip. Ask it last.

**Direction:** unpolished is *better* here. A slightly awkward real person outperforms a smooth one.

# Tier 2 — objection shorts

**60–90s each.** These are efficient because each one does three jobs at once: kills an objection on the page, targets a real search query, and works as social/GBP content.

| Video | Objection it kills | Search query it targets |
|---|---|---|
| **"Am I too old to learn piano?"** | Age doubt | `too old to learn piano` |
| **"Do I need to read music?"** | The biggest jazz-specific blocker | `do I need to read music to play jazz` |
| **"How much should I practise?"** | Time — the #2 barrier | `how much piano practice adult` |
| **"What if I don't have a piano?"** | A solvable blocker he currently just loses | `keyboard for adult beginner` |
| **"I quit as a kid — back to square one?"** | Sunk-cost despair | `learn piano again as an adult` |
| **"What actually happens in a lesson?"** | Fear of the unknown | — |

### Key content notes

**"How much should I practise?"** — the answer is *"twenty minutes, most days, is plenty."* Say the number out loud. Vagueness here reads as "loads," which loses them.

**"I quit as a kid"** — the answer is genuinely encouraging and most people don't know it: the muscle memory is still there, it comes back far faster than starting cold, *and* an adult understands harmony in a week that took a child a year. He should say this. It's the single most reassuring true fact available to us.

**"What if I don't have a piano?"** — name actual models and an actual budget. Specificity converts; "get a decent weighted keyboard" doesn't.

# Tier 3 — ongoing content

Short "one useful thing" clips — a voicing, a lick, a chord trick.

**Caveat, stated plainly:** this is a treadmill, and it's the national-market fight the strategy says to skip. He will not out-publish the YouTube piano educators, and trying is a great way to burn a year.

**Do it only if he enjoys it**, and treat it as GBP/social freshness and warmth — *not* as an SEO strategy. Zero obligation.

# Technical spec

| | |
|---|---|
| **Camera** | Phone, 4K, horizontal. Frame with headroom so vertical crops survive. |

| **Captions** | **Mandatory.** Most of this gets watched with the sound off. |
| **Wardrobe** | What he'd actually wear to teach. Not a suit. |

# Mistakes to avoid

- ❌ **Opening with a virtuosic run** — the single most common and most damaging error
- ❌ **Third person or a formal register** — "Jeremy has performed with…" kills it instantly
- ❌ **Reading a script stiffly** — know the beats, then speak. Bullet points on a card behind the camera, never a teleprompter
- ❌ **Backlit by the window** — silhouettes look amateur
- ❌ **Over-production** — polish makes him read as expensive and corporate. Warm and slightly imperfect converts better
- ❌ **Too long** — if the welcome video passes 90 seconds, cut
- ❌ **No captions**
- ❌ **Talking about himself instead of them**
