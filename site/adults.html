This will not be like it was at school

A lot of adults didn’t stop because they lacked talent. They stopped because of how they were taught.

  • No exams. Unless you want one, in which case we’ll do it properly.
  • No scales for the sake of scales. Technique serves the music, not the other way round.
  • No recitals and no performing in front of strangers.
  • No homework guilt. Some weeks are busy. I’d rather you came anyway.
  • No starting at page one of a method book aimed at eight-year-olds.

What a lesson actually looks like

An hour, at my place in Brunswick West, on a real acoustic piano.

We usually start with whatever you’ve been working on — you play it, I listen, we fix the bit that’s annoying you. Then we take the next step towards whatever tune you’re aiming at, and I’ll usually show you something small about how the harmony works, because that’s the part that makes everything else easier.

You leave with one or two specific things to try. Not a list.

It’s a conversation more than a class. If something isn’t working, say so and we’ll do it another way.

Roughly how the first three months go

Month one

You play something

Three chords, a simple tune, both hands doing different things. It will sound like music, not like an exercise. This is the month that decides whether you keep going, so I make it count.

Month two

It gets slightly harder

More chords, better voicings, some actual technique. This is also where the novelty wears off and life gets busy — which I know, so we plan for it rather than pretending it won’t happen.

Month three

You start sounding like you

Choices start appearing — how to voice something, where to put the melody, what to leave out. This is where most people realise they’re actually playing the piano.

About practice

Twenty minutes, most days, is plenty.

I mean that literally — it is not a lowered bar to make you feel better. Frequent and short beats rare and long, because what you’re building is a physical habit and habits need repetition, not duration.

If you can only manage ten minutes, do ten. If you miss three days, start again on the fourth without the guilt. The people who get good are the ones who never quite stop, not the ones who practise hardest.

If you want the jazz side of it

This is what I do for a living and it’s the part I most enjoy teaching.

Improvising. Reading a lead sheet. Working out a tune by ear. Understanding why a ii–V–I sounds the way it does and then using it. Reharmonising something you already know so it sounds like your version.

You don’t need to be advanced to start — a blues is three chords and you can improvise over it in your first month. But if you already play a bit and want to go deep into harmony, composition and improvisation, that’s the best conversation you can have with me.
