Hello — I’m Jeremy.

I’m a jazz pianist. I teach because I like it, not because the gigs dried up.

▶
Video 3 — Him Playing A standard, one continuous take. This is where credibility lives.

I play piano for a living around Melbourne — jazz mostly, and the sort of work that means you need to be able to read a chart, follow a singer, and make something up on the spot when it’s required.

I’m also on staff at Performing Arts Headquarters, a private performing arts school in Bacchus Marsh, which is where I spend the first half of my week. The rest of it I teach privately from home in Brunswick West.

What I enjoy most is harmony — how chords work, why a particular voicing sounds the way it does, how to take a tune you know and make it sound like yours. Improvisation and composition come out of the same place. If you ever want to go down that road, I’ll happily go a long way down it with you.

But I’m equally happy getting somebody who hasn’t played since school back to the point where they can sit down and play a song. That’s a genuinely good afternoon’s work, and there’s more skill in it than people assume.

How I teach

I don’t have a method book and I don’t have a curriculum that everybody goes through in the same order. I think that’s why a lot of people stop.

What I do instead is find out what you actually want — a specific song, a style, a skill like playing by ear — and then work backwards to figure out what you need in order to get there. The theory and technique still happen. They just arrive attached to something you care about, which is the difference between learning it and remembering it.

I’m patient about wrong notes. I have never once been surprised by somebody being worse than they expected. Everybody is worse than they expected. That’s just what the first few weeks sound like.
