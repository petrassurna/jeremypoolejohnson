When lessons happen

I teach at a performing arts school for the first half of the week, so private lessons run Wednesday, Thursday and Friday.

Those are full days — morning, afternoon and evening — which means there’s room for people who work standard hours and for people who don’t. Daytime slots are the quietest and easiest to get.

Lessons are weekly at the same time, which is how people actually make progress. If that’s impossible for you, say so and we’ll work something out.

The boring but important bits

Paying

The first lesson is paid when you book. After that, either lesson by lesson or in blocks of five, whichever you prefer. Bank transfer or card.

Cancelling

More than 24 hours’ notice and we’ll reschedule, no charge. Less than that and the lesson is charged, because the slot can’t be refilled. Genuine emergencies are genuine emergencies — just tell me.

Holidays

I don’t charge for weeks you’re away, as long as I know in advance. I take a few weeks off a year and you’ll get plenty of notice.

No lock-in

There’s no contract and no term commitment. If it isn’t working, stop — though I’d appreciate being told rather than ghosted, and I promise not to make it awkward.

Why the first lesson isn’t free

Because free trials waste the thing I have least of, which is evenings.

It’s $50 for 45 minutes — half my usual rate. You get a real lesson, not a consultation, and you’ll leave able to play something you couldn’t before. If we’re not a good fit, that’s completely fine and there’s no follow-up sales pitch.
