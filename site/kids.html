What I care about

Songs they recognise

Children practise music they like and avoid music they don’t. That’s not a discipline problem, it’s just how it works — so we use it rather than fighting it.

Exams are optional

If a student wants AMEB, we’ll prepare properly and they’ll do well. If they don’t, they lose nothing. Grades are one path, not the point.

Proper foundations anyway

Reading, technique, rhythm and theory all get taught — just attached to music they care about, so it goes in without a fight.

A note for parents

Most children who quit an instrument quit because it stopped being fun before it started being satisfying. There’s a gap in there — usually somewhere in the second year — and getting a student across it is most of the job.

The way across is that they can already play something they’re proud of. So that comes first, and the grind comes later, in small doses, once there’s a reason for it.

I’ll tell you honestly how it’s going. If your child isn’t practising and isn’t enjoying it, I’d rather say so than take the money for another six months.

Teenagers

Teenagers usually know exactly what they want to play, and the fastest way to lose them is to ignore that.

If they’re into songwriting, we’ll do harmony and composition. If they’re in a band, we’ll do chords, feel and playing with other people. If they want to audition for a music course, that’s a specific job and I’ve prepared students for it.

Longer lessons tend to suit them better — an hour gives room to actually get somewhere.
