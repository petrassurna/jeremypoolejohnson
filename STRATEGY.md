## The three reframes

### 1. This is not a musician's website. It's a local service business landing page.

The default failure mode for a jazz pianist site is obvious and fatal: moody dark hero, big press photo, discography, gig calendar, embedded SoundCloud, third-person bio. That site is built for bookers and fans. It converts approximately zero students.

The buyer is an adult who's slightly embarrassed to be starting again, or a parent with a credit card and an anxiety. Neither cares about sideman credits. They care: *will this person be patient with me, when can I come, where is it, how much.*

### 2. The site is a closer, not a discoverer.

Most traffic will be people who **already have his name.** Someone says "I learn with Jeremy, he's great" — they google "Jeremy Poole Johnson piano" and land on the site. That's the actual conversion event, and it's a 20-second judgment: *does this look like a real professional, and can I see how to start.*

Category search is real but secondary, and it's won mostly by **Google Business Profile**, not by the website.

### 3. Filter, don't maximise.

"After hours" is the central business fact, not a footnote. If he's at the school during the day, he has maybe 15–20 teachable slots — weekday late afternoon/evening plus Saturday morning. He probably wants 8–15 students.

That means roughly **20–30 qualified enquiries a year.** A tiny number. Consequences:

- We don't need volume. We need *fit*.
- Every unqualified enquiry costs a scarce evening slot in trials and admin.
- So we publish price, location and availability up front. Most teachers hide these and drown in tyre-kickers.
- The trial lesson is **paid**, not free. Free trials burn exactly the resource he can't spare.

## Audience

Three segments, three different pitches:

| Segment | What they want | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Adult returners (30–60)** ← **LEAD** | "I played as a kid, I want to play standards, no exams, no judgment" | Best margin, most loyal, and they book *evenings* — matching his availability exactly |
| Parents of 7–16s | "Will my kid stick with it and not hate it" | Volume |
| Serious / pre-college | Improv, audition and exam prep | Highest rate, lowest volume |

**Why adult returners lead:** they're free after 6pm, they pay for themselves, they actively want a *working jazz pianist* rather than a grade-book teacher, and almost nobody markets to them well. Most teacher sites are 100% kid-facing.

Kids/teens stay on the site as a secondary page — still real revenue and referrals, just not the lead.

## Positioning wedge

He is a **professional jazz pianist who teaches**, not a piano teacher who also gigs. That's the differentiator against every generic classical teacher on the same street.

> Learn to actually **play** — improvise, play by ear, play songs you love — with real fundamentals behind it.

## 1. SEO

Priority order matters more than tactics.

### (a) Google Business Profile — highest ROI by a wide margin, and it comes FIRST

> **Full playbook: [GOOGLE-BUSINESS.md](./GOOGLE-BUSINESS.md)**

Category "Piano Instructor." Hours set to his *actual* after-hours availability. Photos. A systematic review-collection habit. **Ten real reviews will out-earn everything we do on-page.**

**This goes live before a single line of code is written.** It is the only channel in the plan that produces students without the website existing.

> Listing type depends on the delivery model: students-come-to-him means the address is public; travel-to-students allows hiding it but ranks weaker. Don't fudge it — see GOOGLE-BUSINESS.md.

### (b) Own page one for "Jeremy Poole Johnson"

The referral-close path. Nobody competes for his name, so it's cheap to win — and it's where most traffic will actually come from. Site + GBP + a couple of profiles covers it.

### (c) Long-tail adult queries — a few pages, not a blog treadmill

Adults search differently from parents, and much less competitively:

| Query | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| `adult piano lessons [city]` | The money query |
| `jazz piano lessons [city]` | His wedge, thin competition |
| `learn piano again as an adult` | Pure intent, near-zero competition |
| `do I need to read music to play jazz` | Real objection, high intent |
| `how much do piano lessons cost [city]` | Ranks *and* pre-qualifies — double duty with published pricing |

`piano lessons [city]` unqualified is brutal — schools, directories, big budgets, and mostly parent traffic anyway. **Skip it.**

### Do NOT chase online-lesson SEO

"Online jazz piano lessons" is a national fight against people with enormous content budgets and YouTube followings. He loses. Treat online as a **convenience option for local students** — travel, illness, bad weather — not a separate market. This decision saves months of wasted effort.

### Technical

Schema: `LocalBusiness` + `Person` + `Service` + `FAQPage`. Cheap, worth doing.

## 2. Messaging

Everything hinges on correctly naming the adult returner's barriers. In actual order:

1. **Embarrassment** — *"I'm 47, I'll be terrible, and this guy plays professionally."* This is #1 and almost nobody addresses it.
2. **Time** — *"I can't practise an hour a day, so what's the point."*
3. **Age doubt** — *"the window closed."*
4. **Bad memories** — scales, exams, a joyless teacher. Many quit *because of how they were taught.*

### Register: permission-giving, not aspirational

"Unlock your potential / master the piano" is exactly wrong. Closer to:

> ### Adult piano lessons for people who quit at twelve.
> You're not too old. There's no exam. Let's get you playing something you actually like.

The "quit at twelve" line names one specific person — that specificity is what makes a headline convert.

### The key structural move: de-position from childhood piano lessons

*No exams. No scales for the sake of scales. No recitals unless you want one.*

This reframes against the memory that's actually doing the blocking.

### The jazz wedge lands harder with adults than parents

Kids get method books. Adults want to **sit down and play a tune.** "You'll be playing a real song within a month" is powerful *and* — with lead sheets and chords rather than notation — genuinely deliverable. We shouldn't promise it otherwise.

### Proof that works on this audience

1. He's a *working* pianist (aspirational — "I'm learning from someone who really does this")
2. He's taught adults before (safety)

In that order.

## 3. Videos

> **Full shot list and scripts: [VIDEO-BRIEF.md](./VIDEO-BRIEF.md)**

Jeremy can shoot these himself, which makes video the cheapest high-leverage asset we have. Summary below.

> **Critical insight:** the most important video is *not* him playing dazzlingly. Dazzling **raises** the intimidation barrier for this audience. The most important video is him being **disarming.**

Four videos, one half-day shoot:

1. **The welcome video (60–90s)** — face to camera at the piano. Names the fear out loud: *"most of my adult students haven't touched a piano in twenty years — that's normal."* Describes what a first lesson is like. Invites them.
   → **Homepage hero. Highest-converting asset on the entire site.**
2. **The "first month" demo (60s)** — here's a real tune with three chords; this is what you could be playing in four weeks. Concrete proof of the promise.
3. **Him playing, one take (~90s)** — a standard, beautifully. Placed *below* the welcome video. **Permission first, credibility second.**
4. **An adult student on camera** — roughly the viewer's age, saying "I was terrified, it was fine." Peer proof beats authority proof against fear.

### Production notes

- Phone on a tripod, lav mic, window light is genuinely enough.
- **Over-produced video actively hurts** — it makes him read as expensive and corporate.
- **Captions mandatory.** This gets browsed silently, at night, on a phone.
- Vertical cutdowns of #1 and #2 feed GBP and social for free.

## 4. Content

### Core pages (5)

| Page | Job |
|---|---|
| **Home** | Welcome video, the promise, price, availability, book trial. Everything above the fold answers "is this for me." |
| **Lessons for Adults** | The money page — carries the weight |
| **About Jeremy** | Short. First person. Human, not a press bio. His playing life is *proof*, not the subject. |
| **Pricing & Availability** | Rates, trial offer, actual evening/Saturday slots, cancellation policy |
| **Book a Trial** | The conversion page |

Plus a secondary **Kids & Teens** page, and the 3–4 long-tail trust pages from the SEO section.

### The "Lessons for Adults" page

The de-positioning, what a lesson actually looks like, what months 1–3 look like, and the practice reality — *"twenty minutes most days is enough"* — which defuses barrier #2 directly.

### Tone

First person. Plain. No pedagogy jargon. Short sentences.

> **Test:** if he'd be embarrassed to say the sentence out loud to a nervous 45-year-old, cut it.

### Governing principle

**Answer every objection on the page.** This audience is shy and will not email to ask a question they think is stupid — they'll just close the tab.

## 5. Contact system

> **The single biggest lever: delete the email step.** A nervous adult will not compose an introductory email confessing they're bad at piano. That blank textarea is where conversions die.

### Recommendation: direct online booking

Cal.com or Calendly, showing only his real after-hours slots, synced to Google Calendar so double-booking is impossible.

**Take payment at booking** via Stripe. This does three things: filters, kills no-shows on scarce evening slots, and removes an awkward money conversation.

### Booking form — keep it short

- Name, email, phone
- *"Have you played before?"*
- *"Anything you'd love to be able to play?"*
- *"Do you have a piano or keyboard at home?"*

The middle two double as rapport and lesson prep. The last one surfaces a solvable blocker he currently just loses.

### Fallback ladder

booking widget → short form → **SMS/WhatsApp** → phone

Feature SMS prominently. Plenty of adults will text who'd never email.

### Follow-up automation

- Confirmation email that reduces anxiety: what to bring, where to park, *"you don't need to prepare anything"*
- Reminder 24h before

Both are template work. Both cut no-shows measurably.

## 6. The initial offer

The highest-leverage single decision on the site.

### Recommendation: a paid first lesson at ~half rate, no obligation

- **Not free** — free trials burn his scarcest resource on non-committal people, devalue him against a professional rate, and no-show heavily. A price is a commitment device.
- **Not full price** — too big a step for someone whose primary barrier is embarrassment.

**Name it rather than calling it a trial:** a **"First Lesson"** or **"Come and Play"** session. 45 minutes. *"We'll work out what you want to play and whether we're a good fit — no obligation."* That last phrase does real work here.

Next to the booking button:

> **"Haven't played in twenty years? That's most of my students."**

### The retention offer matters more than acquisition

No lock-in, but a **5-lesson block at a small saving.** Adults quit at weeks 4–8, when novelty fades and it gets hard. A block carries them past that.

**Filling twelve slots is a retention problem, not a traffic problem.**

### On rate

Adults are far less price-sensitive than parents and will pay a premium for a genuinely good jazz musician. He should price **above** the generic local piano teacher, not at parity.

Published pricing only works as a filter if the number is confident.

## 7. Launch & demand generation

> **This section is the one most likely to determine whether any of it works.** Sections 1–6 cover *converting people who find him*. This covers **how anyone finds him at all in month one** — before SEO has had time to do anything.

### The single highest-leverage action: his existing network

He's a working jazz musician and a music school teacher. He already knows hundreds of relevant people. **One round of personal messages will almost certainly outperform the first six months of SEO**, and it costs nothing.

Who to message:

- Former **private** students, and parents of former students
- Fellow musicians — they get asked "do you know a piano teacher?" constantly
- Venue and gig contacts
- Former colleagues from previous schools
- Friends who've ever said "I wish I'd kept it up"

**Send individual messages, not a BCC blast.** A mass email reads as spam and gets ignored; a personal message gets forwarded. Fifty individual messages is one evening's work and it's the best-value evening in the whole plan.

**The ask is not "book a lesson."** It's "keep me in mind" — far lower friction, and referral is what actually produces students.

> **Draft:**
>
> Hi [name] — quick one. I've started taking a few private piano students in the evenings alongside the school work. Mostly adults who played as kids and want to get back into it, plus some jazz and improvising.
>
> No pressure at all, but if you know anyone who's ever said they'd like to learn — or pick it back up — I'd really appreciate you passing this on: [link]
>
> Cheers!

**Timing:** send this *after* the booking link works, not before. A warm lead who lands on a "coming soon" page is a lead lost permanently.

### Referral partners — the local ecosystem

Ranked by actual value:

| Partner | Why |
|---|---|
| **Piano tuners** | **The best of the lot.** They stand in the homes of people who own pianos and don't play them well. A tuner who likes him refers for years. Offer reciprocity — he sends them tuning work. |
| **Choir directors & community choirs** | Exceptional match. Full of adults who love music, can't play an instrument, and have already proven they'll commit to a weekly evening thing. |
| **Music shops & piano dealers** | Especially anyone selling keyboards to adult beginners — that customer has *just* decided to learn. |
| **Other piano teachers** | Ones who are full, or who teach classical and get jazz enquiries they don't want. Overflow referral is real and reciprocal. |
| **Community bands, big bands, jazz jams** | Adults already playing who want piano or theory. |
| **Venues where he plays** | See below. |
| **U3A / adult education / lifestyle groups** | Directly targets the lead demographic. |

### The gig-to-student pipeline

He plays in public. People approach him after sets — that is the warmest possible lead, and most musicians waste it entirely.

- Carry cards with the booking URL, or a QR code
- An occasional light mention from the stage
- A link in his performer bios and event listings

Costs nothing. Converts unusually well, because they've just watched him play and are already impressed.

### What NOT to do

- ❌ **Paid ads.** He needs ~25 enquiries a year. Piano-lesson CPC is expensive and it competes against a free channel that works better. Revisit only if the free channels are exhausted.
- ❌ **Marketplace directories** (Superprof, TakeLessons and similar). They commoditise him, take a cut, and drop him into a price race against teenagers charging a third of his rate. *Possibly* worth one to seed early students — but eyes open, and never as the main channel.
- ❌ **Social media as a primary channel.** Slow, low intent, and a treadmill. Fine as a supporting surface; a bad place to put the effort.
- ⚠️ **Flyers** are mostly low-ROI — *except* in genuinely targeted places: music shop counters, choir noticeboards, community centres. Cheap enough to test.

### First 90 days

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–2** | Confirm delivery model + contract check. **GBP created and verified.** |
| **Weeks 3–4** | Seed first 5–10 reviews from former private students. Shoot video. |
| **Weeks 5–6** | Site copy, build, booking + Stripe live. |
| **Week 7** | **Network message goes out** — the single biggest day in the plan. |
| **Weeks 8–12** | Referral partner outreach. Review flow becomes routine. First students land. |

### The number that actually matters

**Retention, not acquisition.** If students stay 6 months he needs ~24 new ones a year. If they stay 3 years he needs 4.

Nothing in this document matters as much as whether people stay — which is why the 5-lesson block and the weeks 4–8 drop-off point in section 6 deserve more attention than they usually get.

## Risk: the music school contract

He's employed by a music school and wants private students after hours. Many teaching contracts carry **non-solicitation clauses** covering the school's students.

He should check before we name the school on the site or do anything that reads like trawling their roster. Naming the school is great credibility — but that's his call with the contract in front of him, not ours to make for him.

## Measurement

He needs to know it works:

- Unique booking link + phone number so attribution is possible
- Ask every enquirer **"how did you hear about me?"** on the booking form
- Track: enquiries → trials booked → trials attended → converted to ongoing

With ~20–30 enquiries a year, analytics dashboards are noise. The conversion counts are what matter.
